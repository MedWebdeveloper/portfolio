Reading crypto charts: candlesticks, trends & indicators

📊

Price charts can look like chaos at first glance — a wall of red and green bars moving in every direction. But once you learn to read them, charts become a powerful tool for understanding market sentiment and making smarter decisions. Let's break down the essentials.

Candlesticks: the language of price

The most common chart type in crypto is the candlestick chart. Each "candle" represents price movement over a set period — one hour, one day, whatever you choose. A single candle tells you four things: the open, close, high and low prices for that period.

  • Green candle: the price closed higher than it opened (bullish).
  • Red candle: the price closed lower than it opened (bearish).
  • The wicks (thin lines above and below) show the highest and lowest points reached.
A candlestick is a snapshot of the battle between buyers and sellers.

Identifying trends

Markets generally move in three ways: up (uptrend), down (downtrend), or sideways (consolidation). An uptrend forms a pattern of higher highs and higher lows; a downtrend does the opposite. Recognizing the current trend is the single most useful skill in chart reading.

Support and resistance

Support is a price level where buying tends to step in and stop a fall. Resistance is where selling tends to cap a rise. These levels aren't magic — they're simply prices where lots of traders have historically acted, creating psychological barriers.

Popular indicators

Indicators are calculations layered on top of price to reveal hidden information. A few worth knowing:

  • Moving Averages (MA): smooth out price to show the underlying trend.
  • RSI: measures momentum, signaling when an asset may be overbought or oversold.
  • Volume: confirms the strength behind a move — big moves on low volume are suspicious.

A word of caution

No indicator predicts the future. Technical analysis is about probabilities, not certainties. The best traders combine chart reading with risk management: never invest more than you can afford to lose, and always define your exit before you enter.
